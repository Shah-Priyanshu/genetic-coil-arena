# Goals

Measurable targets on the way to the end goal: an ecosystem of specialist models coordinated by a command centre and fronted by a translator. Every target is judged with the CommArena harness (baselines, multiple seeds or replicates, 95% CIs, message ablations). Each goal states in advance what would count as failure (kill).

## Where the evidence points (2026-10-27)

- **Learned codes only match good hand-designed codes.**
  - R6, squad, at r = 3 and r = 5: learned 35.4 against hand-coded 34.9 team food, then 27.3 against 27.3.
  - R8 step 2: hand designs reach the oracle at 8 B and above.
- **Raw latent messages between LLM specialists fail.** In R9, hidden states gave 0% exact match. A KV-cache handoff is lossless and cuts latency by 43%, but is about 100,000× larger than JSON.
- **What a typed message carries matters more than its format.** In R10, LLM teams whose schema lacked the per-service metric scores lost to a single model (G4). Once the metric scores were in the schema, a 14B centre reading about 800 tokens of typed reports beat the same model reading 4,000 tokens of raw data (46.7 against 29.2 joint). It still only tied the non-LLM BARO, and zero-shot log specialists made it worse (G4b). A *trained* metric fault specialist sending six fault probabilities in its typed report beats BARO by 23 points of joint accuracy, 18 of them when it never saw the test systems (G4c). Every LLM log specialist on RE2, zero-shot or LoRA-trained, stayed at chance: those logs don't carry the signal. On RE3, whose code-level faults do show up in the logs, LLM log specialists (14B, gemma4:e4b, Qwen3-1.7B) still lose to a plain count of new error templates, by 16 to 31 points against the non-LLM fusion (G4d kill): they flag the faulty service but also many healthy ones, and their suspicion scores don't rank. An LLM as the final reader of the fusion's top-3 shortlist loses too (14B −3.2, 30B −8.1; G4e kill): readers mostly copy the ranker, and when they override it they break more correct answers than they fix. On 375 fresh RE1 incidents the G4c fault specialist still beats BARO, by 9.3 points of joint accuracy rather than 23: RE1 lacks disk-I/O and socket metrics (G11a partial). A logistic centre trained on RE1 does not beat the rule centre on RE2 (G11b kill). So far the specialists that earn their place are code and small trained models; the LLMs that earn theirs sit at the language edges (translator, summariser).
- **Distilling the translator into Qwen3-1.7B is stuck at 82.8% on the sealed set.** Four times more verified paraphrases raised the dev score to 95.8% but left the sealed score unchanged (R7a v2). The gap is the shift from template-like dev phrasing to hand-written requests, not data volume. The prompted 14B (95.9%) and gemma4:e4b (92.4%, 4.3 GB) remain the translators.
- **A learned centre can follow a GoalSpec,** including on goal combinations never trained on (G7, at matched training). Over 16 runs at 10k generations, with the goal at the centre and the specialists, it reaches 92% of a hand-coded goal-aware centre. With the goal only in the 3-bit messages, 14 of 16 runs do as well (21.5 against 23.1), but 2 failed to train, so the average is 85%.
- **The whole chain runs on real incident data** (R11). One 14B translator routes requests across three domains without an error (273/273). The trained team gives the same answers through the command centre, and the summaries keep every fact. G8 failed on one criterion: four sealed requests gave a day without a month, and the translator read them as the wrong month. In v2 the translator writes down only what the user said and the catalogue resolves partial dates, asking when they are ambiguous. With that change the 14B passes every criterion (G8b): 92.5% exact match, an NL task score equal to the gold run, and one silently wrong answer in 142 requests. The desk then grew to a new system, Train Ticket, with no prompt edits once every system name in the prompts was rendered from a table (G10): routing stayed at 100% and Train Ticket specs were 90.6% exact, but a habit of adding unstated years held overall exact match at 88% (not passed). A deterministic check of the translator's literals against the request text fixes that habit (G10b): 14B exact match 84.5% → 97.6% on a fresh sealed set, 11 fixed and none broken, silent wrong answers 5 → 0. That is 30B-level accuracy at 14B latency.
- **Growth works by copying.** In R7, a newcomer initialised from an existing specialist joins at zero training cost under either protocol. A from-scratch newcomer is costly under both, because it must learn the skill, not the language. On real incidents (G9 kill), a log-novelty specialist written in code joins the running team without retraining and adds 22.6 points on code-level faults, but costs 3.3 significant points on the old resource faults: on packet loss the services that call the faulty one log the errors, and the centre's rule cannot tell the two cases apart from counts. Making the newcomer credit errors to the service they name did not help (G9b kill), and on fresh Train Ticket incidents the cost appears again (−4.4).

Working design:
- a **typed, machine-native core protocol**: schema-validated GoalSpecs and messages;
- **KV handoff** as a fast path between specialists on the same host;
- **learned compression** only where bandwidth binds;
- **new specialists initialised from existing ones**: LoRA from LoRA on the shared base.

## Compute plan

The runner (`scripts/gpu_runner.py`, `docs/gpu_runner.md`) executes queued jobs on the PC (28 threads, 12 GB GPU, 32 GB RAM).

| Lane | What runs there | Budget |
|---|---|---|
| cpu | numpy and numba sweeps: R8 learned codes, squad ES runs, CI test runs | ≤ 24 workers in total |
| gpu | LLM work: translator evaluations (Ollama), LoRA training and evaluation (Qwen3-1.7B) | one job; `set_per_process_memory_fraction(0.85)` |

Both lanes run at once. Sweeps are split into many jobs, so that the backlog keeps the PC busy while no Claude session is active.
